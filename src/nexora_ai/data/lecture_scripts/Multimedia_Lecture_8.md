## Segment 2 — What Is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial intelligence is the study and development of computational systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence — understanding language, recognizing images, making decisions. Why do we want it? Because AI automates routine work, operates where humans cannot or should not, processes more data than any person could, and assists us everywhere — virtual assistants, chatbots, medical aids, delivery robots. In multimedia specifically, AI is the engine behind content understanding, recommendation, generation, and interaction — including the professor speaking to you right now.

## Segment 3 — Fields and Paradigms

AI spans many fields. Natural language processing lets machines work with text and speech; computer vision interprets images and video; robotics couples perception with action; and machine learning supplies the learning core under all of them. Machine learning itself has three great paradigms: supervised learning, where models learn from labeled examples; unsupervised learning, which finds structure in unlabeled data; and reinforcement learning, where agents learn by trial, error, and reward. Most multimedia AI you will meet is some combination of these.

## Segment 4 — Evaluation: How Good Is the Model?

A model is only useful if we can trust it — so evaluation is its own discipline. Data is split into training and test sets so we measure performance on examples the model never saw. For classification we watch accuracy, precision, recall, and the confusion matrix; for regression, errors like mean squared error. The traps to avoid are overfitting — memorizing training data instead of generalizing — and biased data, which teaches the model the world's mistakes as if they were rules. Good evaluation is what separates a demo from a system.

## Segment 5 — Large Language Models

Large language models are a leap inside natural language processing: networks trained on enormous text corpora that learn to predict, generate, and reason about language. Systems like the one drafting these very words can answer questions, summarize documents, translate between languages, and converse — because predicting the next word well turns out to encode a remarkable amount of knowledge and reasoning. Their limits matter too: they can be confidently wrong, they reflect their training data's biases, and they need care when used where truth matters.

## Segment 6 — Multimodal AI and Digital Twins

The frontier is multimodal AI — models that take several kinds of input at once: text, images, audio, and video together. This closes the loop with our whole course: multimodal interaction gives humans richer interfaces; multimodal AI gives machines richer understanding. Connect it to digital twins and the ambition sharpens — an AI twin that understands sensor data, media streams, and context well enough to act reliably on behalf of its real-world counterpart. That convergence — sensing, media, interaction, learning — is where this course has been pointing all along.
